About us

Who we are

We’re a small communication consulting group based in Boston. We work with small businesses, like inns, restaurants, shops, and offices, when communication has broken down or a big change has left people unsure where they stand.

Every project starts the same way: we listen. We talk with everyone, from the front desk to the owner’s office, to understand what’s really going on. Then we help put simple, lasting fixes in place: clearer messages, better ways to speak up, and everyday habits that bring a team back together.

The basics

Two ideas behind everything we do

You’ll hear us use these terms a lot. Here’s what they mean, in plain language.

Organizational communication

Organizational communication is how people in a workplace share information, ideas, and concerns with each other: in meetings, emails, group chats, notes on the break-room board, and quick conversations in the hallway.

Part of it is getting the message across, so the right people hear the right thing, clearly and on time. But it goes deeper than that. The way people talk to each other every day shapes what the organization becomes. Where questions are welcome, people speak up. Where news travels by rumor, people stop trusting what they hear.

Everyday example

Four roommates share an apartment. A chore chart on the fridge tells everyone whose turn it is to take out the trash. That’s the message getting across. But if they only vent about each other behind closed doors, the apartment starts to feel tense, and soon that’s just what living there is like. The way they talk shapes the home itself.

Organizational culture

Organizational culture is the shared sense of “how we do things around here.” It’s the values, habits, and unwritten rules people pick up just by being part of a group: what gets praised, what gets ignored, and what nobody would ever dare to do.

You won’t find it in the employee handbook. You see it in how people treat each other on an ordinary Tuesday, and it’s what new hires figure out in their first week.

Everyday example

Picture two coffee shops on the same street. At one, the staff joke around, cover each other’s breaks, and the manager thanks everyone at the end of a shift. At the other, people keep their heads down and only hear from the manager when something goes wrong. Same coffee, same prices, completely different places to work. That difference is culture.
